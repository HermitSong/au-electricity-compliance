# D09 - Environment and Planning Approvals for Solar, BESS, and Network Projects

Solar, BESS, and electricity network projects require a layered approval analysis:

1. Commonwealth environment law under the EPBC Act and the 2025-2026 reform package.
2. State planning approval, which differs materially across all eight states and territories.
3. Fire-safety referral and design requirements, especially for BESS.
4. Noise, glint and glare, traffic, land use, biodiversity, cultural heritage, decommissioning, and waste/recycling conditions.

Do not infer a planning pathway from NEM market registration. A BESS that is registered with AEMO may still need a state planning consent, EPBC referral, fire-safety study, and decommissioning conditions.

## 1. Commonwealth environment layer

The Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (Cth) applies nationally. A project must be referred if it is likely to have a significant impact on a Matter of National Environmental Significance (MNES). Solar, BESS, and transmission projects most commonly encounter MNES risk through listed threatened species, listed threatened ecological communities, listed migratory species, Ramsar wetlands, National Heritage, World Heritage, and Commonwealth land.

The project pathway is separate from state planning. A state consent does not remove the need for an EPBC referral where the significant-impact test is met.

### 2026 reform status

Official Commonwealth reform materials state:

- Environment Protection Reform Bills passed Parliament on 28 November 2025.
- Some provisions commenced immediately and tranche 1 commenced on 20 February 2026.
- The National Environmental Protection Agency was established on 1 July 2026.
- Environment Information Australia also commenced on 1 July 2026.
- The first four National Environmental Standards were made on 19 August 2026.
- Remaining reforms are to commence on or before 1 December 2026.

Current-law note: because the reform package is staged, project advice must identify whether the relevant EPBC decision, referral, audit, approval, surrender, or enforcement step is governed by the old provision, a commenced reform provision, or a transitional provision.

## 3. NSW pathway

### State Significant Development for electricity generating works

Under the EP&A Act 1979 and SEPP (Planning Systems) 2021, electricity generating works can become SSD where capital investment value exceeds the relevant threshold. The commonly used thresholds for electricity generating works are CIV greater than $30 million, or greater than $10 million in an environmentally sensitive area of State significance. BESS is commonly assessed as part of electricity generating works or storage infrastructure, depending on project structure and consent drafting.

SSD process: request SEARs, prepare EIS, exhibition, submissions, Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure assessment, and determination by the Minister, delegate, or Independent Planning Commission where applicable.

### Network projects, SSI, and CSSI

Do not treat network projects as generation projects. Transmission and distribution works by public authorities/network operators may be assessed under Part 5 or declared as State Significant Infrastructure. A project can be declared Critical State Significant Infrastructure, which changes appeal and decision mechanics. The NSW Transmission Guideline applies to large-scale transmission infrastructure declared SSI and should be used separately from solar/BESS guidelines.

### NSW Renewable Energy Planning Framework

Official NSW materials identify a framework that includes:

- Large-Scale Solar Energy Guideline, covering landscape and visual assessment, agricultural land use, decommissioning, and glint and glare.
- Transmission Guideline, covering large-scale transmission infrastructure, undergrounding issues, bushfire risk, and landscape/visual impacts.
- Benefit Sharing Guideline for host communities.

Current-law caveat: benefit-sharing rates and units for standalone BESS should be checked against the current guideline text at the time of advice.

## 4. Victoria pathway

Victoria uses the Planning and Environment Act 1987 and Victoria Planning Provisions.

Key controls:

- Clause 53.13 Renewable energy facility sets application requirements and decision guidelines for renewable energy facilities.
- Larger utility-scale generation and storage projects commonly have the Minister for Planning as responsible authority; smaller projects can remain with council depending on the planning scheme and facility type.
- Planning Victoria guidance for solar energy facilities covers site selection, landscape and visual impact, agricultural land, glint and glare, decommissioning, and model permit conditions.
- CFA fire-safety design guidance and model requirements are commonly used for renewable energy facility permit conditions.
- Development Facilitation Program and related VPP controls can accelerate some renewable projects, but eligibility must be confirmed against the current VPP text.

Victoria planning does not replace D16 market entry or D07 electrical safety. A BESS may simultaneously need a planning permit, ESC generation licence/exemption analysis, ESV complex electrical installation compliance, AEMO registration, connection approval, and fire-safety conditions.

## 5. South Australia pathway

South Australia uses the Planning, Development and Infrastructure Act 2016 and the Planning and Design Code.

Typical renewable energy facilities in supportive rural or remote zones are often performance assessed. Sensitive overlays can convert a project into restricted development, including possible State Planning Commission involvement and more difficult approval mechanics. Crown development under PDI Act s 131 can be available for projects promoted by or on behalf of the Crown and has been used for major energy infrastructure. Impact assessed development remains available for projects declared or prescribed for that pathway, but it is not the default pathway for ordinary solar/BESS projects.

SA project teams should coordinate planning with OTR infrastructure technical regulation, CFS referral/fire safety expectations, native vegetation, cultural heritage, road access, and network connection requirements.

## 6. BESS fire-safety layer

Fire-safety requirements are often embedded in planning conditions rather than issued as a standalone "fire licence".

| Source | Jurisdiction / publisher | Role in approvals |
|---|---|---|
| Fire and Rescue NSW BESS fire safety study and technical guidance | NSW | Commonly used in SSD conditions for BESS hazard analysis, thermal runaway controls, water supply, emergency response, separation distances, and consultation with FRNSW. |
| CFA Design Guidelines and Model Requirements - Renewable Energy Facilities | VIC | Used for Victorian permit conditions, site access, water supply, vegetation management, emergency planning, and fire authority consultation. |
| AFAC Large-scale BESS installations guideline | National fire and emergency services reference | Provides national fire-service expectations for large BESS installations; often informs state fire authority comments. |
| AS/NZS 5139:2019 | Standards Australia / Standards New Zealand | Directly focused on battery systems for use with power conversion equipment in buildings and related contexts. Utility-scale BESS often uses it as a reference alongside NFPA 855, UL 9540A, manufacturer data, and fire authority guidance. |
| CFS and local fire authority requirements | SA and other states | Applied through referral comments, planning conditions, emergency management plans, and operational fire plans. |

## 8. Battery stewardship and waste

Battery waste and recycling obligations are moving quickly.

NSW has made the Product Lifecycle Responsibility Regulation 2026. NSW EPA materials describe the move toward a mandatory battery product stewardship scheme from 1 October 2026, focused on regulated batteries and products within the regulation. Utility-scale BESS battery packs should not be assumed to be captured by the same small or portable battery category without checking the definitions.

For utility BESS, practical controls usually remain:

- decommissioning and rehabilitation conditions in the planning consent;
- OEM take-back or recycling commitments in supply contracts;
- dangerous goods and waste transport compliance;
- fire-damaged battery handling procedure; and
- evidence that the project has a credible end-of-life pathway.

## 9. Enforcement posture

EPBC enforcement, state planning compliance, and fire-safety condition compliance are separate. Common public enforcement themes in renewable projects include unauthorised clearing, works commencing before pre-construction conditions are satisfied, non-compliance with approved plans, failure to implement environmental management controls, and failure to meet decommissioning or rehabilitation commitments.

Current-law caution: do not invent named solar/BESS EPBC penalty examples unless a current official decision, register entry, court order, or regulator release is available. Use official registers and court materials for enforcement claims.

## 11. Current-law caveats

- EPBC reforms are in staged commencement through 2026. Always check transitional provisions for live matters.
- NSW benefit-sharing rates, especially for standalone BESS, should be confirmed against the current guideline rather than copied from summaries.
- Victoria Development Facilitation Program eligibility for renewable energy projects depends on current VPP text and ministerial directions.
- SA restricted-development triggers depend on the current Planning and Design Code zone and overlay mapping for the exact parcel.
- Battery stewardship is changing. Utility-scale BESS should not be treated as captured by small/portable battery schemes unless definitions clearly include it.
