# D08 - Work Health and Safety for Electricity and Renewable Energy Projects

WHS obligations attach because a person conducts a business or undertaking, manages a workplace, designs plant, supplies equipment, installs assets, or sends workers to site. They do not depend on holding an electricity retail licence, generation licence, AEMO registration, or exemption.

For electricity and renewable energy projects, WHS/OHS sits beside electrical safety law. WHS manages workplace risk; electrical safety law controls licensed electrical work, certificates, electrical incidents, and state electrical regulators. Use D07 for electrical licensing and electrical safety regulators.

## 2. Core duties

### PCBUs and employers

In model WHS jurisdictions, the primary duty holder is the PCBU. Section 19 requires a PCBU to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the health and safety of workers and other persons. A BESS owner, EPC contractor, civil subcontractor, O&M contractor, EMS vendor, or site operator can each be a PCBU.

Victoria uses different language. Under the OHS Act 2004, duties are framed around employers, self-employed persons, persons who manage or control workplaces, designers, manufacturers, suppliers, and officers. The operational result is similar, but the legal labels and offence provisions are not interchangeable.

### Multiple duty holders

Model WHS duties are not transferable and may be held concurrently. PCBUs must consult, cooperate, and coordinate with each other where duties overlap. This is critical for generation and BESS works, where the site owner, EPC, electrical contractor, civil contractor, crane contractor, network service provider, security contractor, and commissioning vendor may all affect the same risk.

### Officers

Officers must exercise due diligence. Under model WHS law, due diligence includes acquiring WHS knowledge, understanding business hazards, ensuring resources and processes, ensuring incident and hazard information is received and acted on, ensuring legal compliance processes, and verifying implementation. In Victoria, officer exposure is dealt with through OHS Act mechanisms, including attribution and workplace manslaughter provisions.

Insurance or indemnity for WHS penalties is restricted or prohibited in several jurisdictions. Do not treat insurance as a substitute for officer due diligence.

## 4. High-risk work and SWMS

High Risk Work licences are separate from electrical work licences. Common classes on renewable energy sites include:

| Class | Activity | Project relevance |
|---|---|---|
| WP | Boom-type elevating work platform with boom length 11 m or more | Substation, inverter station, panel, and communications work |
| DG / RB / RI / RA | Dogging and rigging | Transformers, PCS units, battery containers, switchroom modules |
| CN / C2 / C6 / C1 / C0 | Slewing mobile cranes and other crane classes | Heavy lifts for transformers and BESS containers |
| LF | Forklift operation | Site logistics and warehousing |
| SB / SI / SA | Scaffolding | Elevated work platforms and temporary works |

Working at heights is not itself a High Risk Work licence class, but fall-risk controls and SWMS are still required where regulations trigger them. Do not confuse training certificates such as "work safely at heights" with statutory HRW licences.

For BESS, "work on or near energised electrical installations or services" is the recurring SWMS trigger. Once battery modules and DC systems are installed, full de-energisation may not be a simple lockout exercise. Treat commissioning and fault-finding as high-risk work, not merely software activity.

## 5. Electrical work exclusion

WHS law controls workplace risk from electricity. It does not usually issue electrical contractor licences or completion certificates. Those sit in D07 state electrical safety law.

Examples:

- NSW: electrical licensing and CCEW sit under the Home Building Act and Gas and Electricity (Consumer Safety) framework, while workplace injury and dangerous incident notification sit with SafeWork NSW.
- SA: electrical contractor licensing sits with CBS; technical and installation safety sits with OTR; workplace notifiable incidents sit with SafeWork SA.
- VIC: electrical licensing, COES, licensed electrical inspector requirements, and complex electrical installation duties sit with ESV; workplace safety duties sit with WorkSafe Victoria.
- QLD: the Electrical Safety Act 2002 is its own statutory track and should be checked alongside WHS duties.

EMS vendors should classify field tasks carefully. Low-voltage supply wiring, CT/VT secondary wiring, cabinet power supplies, and energised testing may be electrical work or high-risk work even if the commercial scope is described as software commissioning.

## 6. Incident notification

In model WHS jurisdictions, notifiable incidents include death, serious injury or illness, and dangerous incidents arising out of work. Electric shock, arc flash, battery fire, explosion, structural collapse, fall from height, uncontrolled release of gas or chemicals, and contact with overhead powerlines can each trigger notification depending on facts.

Timing under model WHS laws is generally immediate notification after becoming aware, by the fastest possible means, with written notice within 48 hours if the regulator requests it. The incident site must be preserved except for rescue, making the site safe, or regulator-authorised disturbance.

Victoria has comparable immediate notification and site preservation duties under the OHS Act 2004, but the statutory references are Victorian OHS references, not WHS Act section numbers.

For BESS and electrical incidents, do not stop at WHS notification. Check D07 for electrical safety notification to ESV, OTR, ESO, Building and Energy, or the local electrical regulator.

## 7. Penalties and enforcement

### Model WHS and state penalty caution

Safe Work Australia publishes model-law maximum monetary penalties, including indexed figures. Those figures are not automatically the current penalty in every state. Each jurisdiction must be checked for adoption, indexation, and amendments.

Known current anchors:

- SA industrial manslaughter: SafeWork SA states the offence commenced on 1 July 2024, with maximum penalties of 20 years imprisonment for individuals and $18 million for companies.
- NSW industrial manslaughter: NSW introduced the offence in 2024, with a maximum $20 million penalty for a body corporate, 25 years imprisonment for an individual, and no limitation period.
- VIC workplace manslaughter: WorkSafe Victoria states the offence commenced on 1 July 2020. OHS Act s 39G carries a maximum of 25 years imprisonment for individuals and 100,000 penalty units for bodies corporate.
- Model-law category 1, 2, and 3 penalty tables must be reconciled with the relevant state statute before formal advice.

### Electricity and renewable project enforcement examples

| Case | Jurisdiction | Outcome | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| LH Holding Management Pty Ltd workplace manslaughter | VIC | WorkSafe Victoria reported a $1.3 million fine in 2024 for Victoria's first workplace manslaughter conviction | Small companies and owner-operated businesses can face workplace manslaughter liability. |
| PV Solar Pro Pty Ltd | NSW | SafeWork NSW reported a $300,000 fine after a young apprentice died falling through a skylight during solar installation | Rooftop solar remains a high-priority falls enforcement area. |
| JD Finlay Electrical Pty Ltd service-pit death | SA | SafeWork SA reported a $300,000 fine after a worker died while pulling electrical cables through conduit into a pit | Electrical contracting work still requires confined-space, pit, training, and safe-system controls. |
| QLD electrical safety prosecutions | QLD | OWHSP publishes electrical safety prosecutions, including six-figure fines for multiple electrical safety breaches | Electrical safety can be prosecuted through a specialised electrical safety statute and prosecution pathway. |

## 8. Implementation checklist

1. Map WHS/OHS law by state before reusing templates.
2. For Victoria, convert PCBU language to OHS Act roles and cross-reference D07 ESV duties.
3. Appoint the principal contractor in writing and align actual site control with the contract.
4. Require SWMS for energised work, BESS DC work, heavy lifts, falls, excavations, traffic management, and mobile plant interfaces.
5. Check HRW licences separately from electrical licences.
6. Build incident response matrices with two columns: WHS/OHS regulator and electrical safety regulator.
7. Maintain officer due diligence evidence: board reports, risk registers, budget approvals, audit findings, corrective action tracking, and verification.
8. Treat battery fire, electric shock, arc flash, and uncontrolled thermal events as immediate escalation events.

## 10. Current-law caveats

- State adoption of 2023 model WHS penalty uplifts is not uniform. Do not quote Safe Work Australia model penalty figures as NSW, SA, QLD, WA, TAS, ACT, or NT law without checking the state statute.
- Victoria is not a harmonised model WHS jurisdiction. PCBU wording is useful for comparison only and should not be used as the legal label in Victorian advice.
- Industrial manslaughter provisions are now widespread but differ by fault test, penalty, limitation period, and prosecution practice.
- Electrical incident response must cross-check D07. WHS notification alone may not satisfy ESV, OTR, ESO, or Building and Energy reporting rules.
