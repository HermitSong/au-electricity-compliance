# D12 - Energy Ombudsman Schemes and Consumer Protection Overlay

Energy retailers, distributors and many exempt sellers must give customers access to an approved dispute resolution pathway. For NECF jurisdictions, ombudsman membership is a legal operating condition rather than a customer-service preference.

This page covers ombudsman access, exempt sellers, embedded networks, complaint referrals and the consumer-law overlay. It is not a complete state-by-state manual for all consumer protection laws.

## Scheme overview

| jurisdiction | scheme / forum | legal basis | binding decision note | customer cost | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NSW | Energy & Water Ombudsman NSW (EWON) | NERL s 86 and scheme membership rules | EWON increased its binding decision limit to $100,000 | Free for consumers | Relevant to authorised retailers, distributors and many exempt entities. |
| Victoria | Energy & Water Ombudsman Victoria (EWOV) | ESC licence conditions and Victorian exemption framework | EWOV states $20,000, or $50,000 with both parties' agreement | Free for consumers | Victoria is not a NECF jurisdiction. |
| South Australia | Energy & Water Ombudsman SA (EWOSA) | NERL s 86 and ESCOSA/industry scheme arrangements | Check EWOSA charter for current limit before quoting | Free for consumers | Relevant to AER-authorised retailers operating in SA. |
| Queensland | Energy and Water Ombudsman Queensland (EWOQ) | Energy and Water Ombudsman Act 2006 (Qld) | Check Act/regulations for binding powers before quoting | Free for consumers | Embedded network participants may be automatic scheme participants below the customer threshold described by EWOQ. |
| Tasmania | Energy Ombudsman Tasmania | Energy Ombudsman Act 1998 (Tas) | Check Act for current determination powers before quoting | Free for consumers | Statutory ombudsman model. |
| Western Australia | Energy and Water Ombudsman WA (EWOWA) | WA licensing and scheme framework | Public scheme materials identify $20,000 and $50,000 with provider agreement | Free for consumers | WA is outside the NECF. |
| ACT | ACAT energy and water cases | ACT tribunal jurisdiction | Tribunal orders rather than industry ombudsman binding-decision model | Low-cost tribunal pathway | ACT does not use a separate energy ombudsman scheme in the same way as NSW, Victoria, SA or Queensland. |
| Northern Territory | Utilities Commission for alleged licence, code or regulatory breaches; Ombudsman NT for Jacana Energy or Power and Water complaints; NT Consumer Affairs for Rimfire Energy complaints | Electricity Reform Act 2000, Electricity Retail Supply Code and the remit of each complaint body | Route depends on the provider and whether the issue is an individual dispute or alleged regulatory non-compliance | Complaint bodies are generally free for consumers | The NT is outside the NECF. The customer must first use the retailer or network provider's complaint process. |

## Who must join

### Authorised retailers and distributors

NERL s 86 requires retailers and distributors in NECF jurisdictions to be members of an energy ombudsman scheme approved or recognised for the jurisdiction. AER retailer authorisation should be read together with scheme membership and complaint-handling readiness.

Victoria, Western Australia and the Northern Territory use separate local licensing and dispute-resolution arrangements. In the NT, Jacana Energy and Power and Water complaints route to Ombudsman NT, Rimfire Energy complaints route to NT Consumer Affairs, and alleged licence or code breaches may be raised with the Utilities Commission after the provider complaint process. An AER authorisation does not remove local requirements.

### Exempt sellers and embedded networks

Exempt sellers and embedded network operators are not outside consumer protection. AER's exempt selling framework and jurisdictional scheme rules require many residential exempt sellers and embedded network operators to join the relevant dispute resolution scheme where the scheme can accept them.

| control | reason |
|---|---|
| Confirm scheme eligibility before selling to residents. | Some exemption categories require membership or evidence of steps taken to join. |
| Keep scheme details on customer bills where required. | AER embedded network reforms introduce stronger customer-information requirements. |
| Maintain contact, ownership and customer-count information. | AER and schemes use this information for exemption compliance and complaint access. |
| Treat ombudsman referrals as compliance signals. | Referrals can lead to AER or state regulator action. |

## Embedded network review milestones

AER's embedded network review strengthens access to consumer protections. Treat the following as implementation milestones, subject to checking the final instrument and transition provisions before operational use:

| date | change | compliance implication |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-01-01 | New embedded network configurations can no longer rely on specified deemed exemption categories under AER's reform direction. | New projects should assess registrable exemption, individual exemption or retailer authorisation pathways. |
| 2026-07-01 | Exempt sellers with ombudsman obligations must include scheme information on bills; some exempt sellers must publish customer tariffs. | Billing templates and exemption registers need updates before commencement. |

## Australian Consumer Law overlay

The Australian Consumer Law (ACL), in Schedule 2 to the Competition and Consumer Act 2010, applies alongside energy-specific law. ACCC and state or territory consumer regulators may enforce it.

| ACL area | energy retail relevance |
|---|---|
| Misleading or deceptive conduct | Discounts, reference-price comparisons, "cheaper" claims and plan comparisons must be substantiated. |
| Unconscionable conduct | High-pressure conduct toward vulnerable customers may trigger ACL risk in addition to energy-law risk. |
| Unsolicited consumer agreements | Door-to-door and telephone sales have time, disclosure, cooling-off and leave-on-request requirements. |

Official ACCC energy sales cases include:

| entity | official outcome | conduct area | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| AGL Sales / AGL SA | Federal Court penalties totalling $1.555 million | Door-to-door sales and misleading representations | https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/1-million-in-penalties-for-door-to-door-sales |
| Origin Energy | Federal Court penalty $2 million | Unlawful door-to-door sales tactics | https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/origin-to-pay-2-million-for-unlawful-door-to-door-sales-tactics |
| EnergyAustralia | Federal Court consent penalty $1.2 million | Unlawful sales tactics including do-not-knock conduct | https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/energyaustralia-ordered-by-consent-to-pay-12-million-for-unlawful-sales-tactics |
| Door-to-door salespeople generally | Federal Court confirmed salespeople must not ignore do-not-knock signs | ACL unsolicited sales compliance | https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/court-confirms-salespeople-must-not-ignore-do-not-knock-signs |

EIC under the NERL is separate from ACL consent and unsolicited-sales rules. A retailer can have ACL risk and still separately fail NERL explicit informed consent requirements.

## Telemarketing, Do Not Call and spam

Energy sales and comparison channels may also trigger ACMA-administered regimes:

| regime | core obligation |
|---|---|
| Do Not Call Register Act 2006 | Marketing lists must be checked against the register at least every 30 days; marketing calls to registered numbers are restricted. |
| Telemarketing and Research Calls Industry Standard | Calling hours, caller identification and termination-on-request rules apply. |
| Spam Act 2003 | Commercial electronic messages require consent, sender identification and unsubscribe facilities. |

Official ACMA materials should be checked before quoting current maximum penalties, because penalty settings can change.

## Embedded network controls

| control | why it matters |
|---|---|
| Confirm retail exemption category and conditions before selling electricity. | Exemption conditions can include price, billing, disconnection, information and ombudsman obligations. |
| Join or take documented steps to join the relevant ombudsman scheme before serving residents where required. | CAM Engineering shows non-membership can lead to Federal Court penalties. |
| Put ombudsman details on bills where required. | AER embedded network reforms make dispute-resolution access more visible. |
| Maintain hardship, life support, disconnection and complaint processes even when exempt. | Exemption is not immunity from consumer protection. |
| Track ownership and operator changes. | Scheme and AER records must remain current. |

## Coverage caveats

- Binding decision limits for EWOSA, EWOQ and Tasmania should be checked against the current charter or legislation before quoting in advice.
- This page does not provide a full ACL, Privacy Act, CDR, DNCR or Spam Act compliance program.
- Ombudsman annual complaint counts change every reporting year. Use the scheme's current annual report for live figures.
