# D16 - Victoria's Separate Energy Regulation Regime

Victoria is the key NEM exception for retail energy regulation. It participates in the NEM wholesale and network framework, but it has not adopted the National Energy Customer Framework for retail customer protections. Victorian retail licensing and customer protection are regulated by the Essential Services Commission Victoria (ESC) under Victorian legislation. Electrical safety is regulated by Energy Safe Victoria (ESV) under the Electricity Safety Act 1998.

Practical rule: AER retailer authorisation is not enough to sell electricity or gas to Victorian retail customers. A retailer needs the Victorian licensing/exemption position under ESC law. AEMO registration is also not a substitute for ESC generation licensing or exemption analysis.

## 2. ESC retail licensing and customer rules

### Retail licence

Electricity Industry Act 2000 (Vic) s 16 prohibits a person from engaging in the regulated activity of selling electricity by retail unless licensed or exempt. Gas retailing has a parallel Victorian gas framework. National retailers commonly hold both AER authorisation for NECF jurisdictions and ESC licences for Victoria, but the legal bases are different.

### Energy Retail Code of Practice

The Energy Retail Code was remade as the Energy Retail Code of Practice with effect from 1 March 2022. ESC materials state that version 6 came into effect on 1 July 2026. ESC's Energy Consumer Reforms materials also identify version 7 changes scheduled for 1 October 2026.

Core Victorian retail controls include:

- best offer messaging on bills;
- payment difficulty assistance;
- life support customer records;
- family violence protections;
- bill information and marketing rules;
- complaint handling and bill review timeframes; and
- disconnection as a last-resort control.

Current-law caveat: version 6 is the current code from 1 July 2026. Version 7 reforms should be described as scheduled or incoming until their commencement date is reached.

### Version 7 reform items to track

ESC's 2025 Energy Consumer Reforms materials identify reforms including an increased minimum debt threshold for disconnection, cheaper-plan transfer protections for some customers, and rules addressing plan names and pricing confusion. Use the current ESC code text before quoting operative obligations after 1 October 2026.

## 3. Victorian Default Offer

The Victorian Default Offer is set by ESC and is not the AER Default Market Offer. VDO prices are set by distribution zone and tariff type.

For VDO 2026-27, ESC's final decision materials state an average annual residential flat-tariff bill of $1,591 for the reference consumption level, a decrease of $84 or 5 percent from 2025-26. Use the ESC final decision schedules for exact distribution-zone and tariff values.

## 4. Wrongful disconnection and payment difficulty

Victoria has a distinctive Wrongful Disconnection Payment regime. If a retailer disconnects a customer without satisfying the Victorian requirements, the customer may be entitled to statutory payments, and ESC can make decisions or take enforcement action. The common reference amount is $500 per day, pro-rated for part days, but always check the current Act and ESC WDP materials for the applicable calculation.

The Payment Difficulty Framework is embedded in the Energy Retail Code of Practice. It includes standard assistance for residential customers and tailored assistance for customers in arrears. In compliance design, disconnection must be treated as a final gated process after best offer, payment difficulty, concession, life support, family violence, and notice requirements have been checked.

## 6. Energy Safe Victoria and electrical safety

ESV regulates Victorian electrical safety under the Electricity Safety Act 1998 and related regulations. For power projects, the most important D16/D07 bridge is the complex electrical installation regime.

### Complex electrical installations and ESMS

ESV guidance states that a complex electrical installation includes an electrical installation with generation capacity equal to or greater than 1000 kVA and expressly includes BESS, solar farms, wind farms, co-generation, rotating/dynamic grid stabilisers, and other generating technologies. A complex installation can also include certain electric lines on land not owned or leased by the owner/operator of the line.

Owners and operators of complex electrical installations must design, construct, operate, maintain, and decommission the installation to minimise, as far as practicable:

- hazards and risks to any person's safety;
- hazards and risks of damage to property; and
- bushfire danger arising from the installation.

Major electricity companies must submit and comply with accepted Electricity Safety Management Schemes. Owners of complex electrical installations can also consider voluntary ESMS pathways where appropriate.

### Bushfire mitigation and REFCL

Victoria's bushfire mitigation controls are shaped by Black Saturday and the Victorian Bushfires Royal Commission findings on electricity-caused fires. ESV oversees bushfire mitigation plans and Rapid Earth Fault Current Limiter (REFCL) compliance for specified zone substations. REFCL-related connection impacts can matter for generators and BESS connecting in affected distribution areas, because equipment may need to tolerate voltage conditions associated with REFCL operation. Confirm engineering requirements with the relevant DNSP.

### Victoria electrical safety enforcement examples

Use D07 for the full table. Key D16 examples include:

- Powercor: $2.1 million fine in 2024 after failures involving inspection of 4866 spans, vegetation clearance, and the Glenmore fire.
- AusNet Services: $200,000 Supreme Court penalty/order after installing and energising a bare 22 kV high-voltage powerline in a highest-bushfire-risk zone.
- Hydroxygas / Renaud Kobrynski: convictions and fines after an unsafe BESS was reconnected contrary to ESV direction.
- Greenova: $9,000 fine without conviction in 2026 for unsafe home battery installations and failure to have BESS assessed by a licensed electrical inspector before connection.

## 7. Generation licensing and General Exemption Order 2022

Electricity Industry Act 2000 (Vic) s 16 also regulates generation for supply or sale. A generator needs an ESC generation licence unless exempt.

The main exemption instrument is the General Exemption Order 2022, made under the Victorian exemption framework and effective from 1 January 2023. The practical threshold used in the KB is:

- total output less than 30 MW at a single connection point to the transmission or distribution system: generally exempt from ESC generation licensing, with registration conditions depending on exemption category; and
- 30 MW or greater: assess ESC generation licence or a specific exemption pathway.

Current-law caveat: BESS treatment under GEO 2022 should be confirmed against the project structure and ESC/DEECA guidance. AEMO registration under the NER is separate and does not replace ESC licensing or exemption analysis.

## 8. Embedded networks

Victorian embedded networks use the Victorian exemption framework, not the AER NECF exemption framework. Exempt sellers and suppliers may need to register on ESC's Register of Exempt Persons and comply with conditions covering price caps or VDO-linked pricing, billing information, EWOV access, disconnection protections, and customer information.

Current-law caveat: Victorian Government policy commitments to restrict or ban new residential embedded networks have been evolving. Check current DEECA and ESC materials before advising on a new apartment project.

## 9. Solar Victoria and emergency backstop

Solar Homes and Solar for Apartments program participation is governed through Solar Victoria program requirements and Notice to Market documents.

Key program controls include:

- installer/designer accreditation requirements through Solar Accreditation Australia where applicable;
- New Energy Tech Consumer Code participation where required;
- approved product lists;
- declarations about serious WorkSafe contraventions or prosecutions; and
- smart inverter and communications requirements where specified.

Victoria's distributed solar emergency backstop applies to new and replacement rooftop solar systems within the stated capacity class from 1 October 2024, subject to DEECA and DNSP implementation documents. The mechanism is designed to allow DNSPs, when instructed for power system security reasons, to remotely reduce or stop exports from eligible systems using communications capability such as CSIP-AUS/IEEE 2030.5.

Current-law caveat: capacity wording can appear as kW or kVA across implementation documents. Use the current DEECA guidance and the relevant DNSP's technical documents for final design.

## 12. Current-law caveats

- Energy Retail Code of Practice version 7 reforms should be treated as scheduled until their commencement date and final operative text are confirmed.
- GEO 2022 application to BESS should be checked against project structure, connection point, output rating, and ESC/DEECA guidance.
- Embedded network reform in Victoria remains policy-sensitive. Confirm current DEECA/ESC implementation status before advising on new residential projects.
- Emergency backstop design must use the current DEECA guidance and the relevant DNSP technical requirements, especially where documents vary between kW and kVA terminology.
- Paid ESC penalty notices should be described as paid notices for alleged contraventions unless the official source states an admission. Court-ordered penalties, such as the Origin 2025 matter, can be described as court outcomes.
