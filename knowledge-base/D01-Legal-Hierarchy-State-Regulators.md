# D01 - Australian Electricity Legal Hierarchy and State Regulators

Australia's national energy laws use a cooperative application-of-laws model. South Australia is the lead legislator for the National Electricity Law, National Energy Retail Law and National Gas Law, and other participating jurisdictions apply those laws through local application legislation.

The electricity market hierarchy is:

`National Electricity Law (NEL) -> National Electricity Rules (NER) -> AEMO procedures and guidelines`.

The NECF retail hierarchy is:

`National Energy Retail Law (NERL) -> National Energy Retail Rules (NERR) -> AER retailer authorisation and compliance`.

Victoria, Western Australia and the Northern Territory do not use the NECF retail framework. State licensing and safety regimes remain separate from AEMO registration and AER authorisation.

## National architecture

| layer | instrument | maker / administrator | practical relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy | Energy and Climate Change Ministerial Council decisions | Commonwealth, state and territory ministers | National law amendment policy, statements of policy principles and reform priorities. |
| Law | NEL, NERL and NGL schedules in South Australian host Acts | South Australian Parliament as lead legislator; applied by participating jurisdictions | Creates market institutions, rule-making powers, civil penalties and retailer authorisation obligations. |
| Rules | NER, NERR and NGR | AEMC | Day-to-day market, network, dispatch, metering, retail and consumer-protection obligations. |
| Procedures | AEMO procedures, standards and guidelines | AEMO under rule-based consultation processes | Technical implementation for dispatch, settlement, metering, data communications, FCAS and registration. |
| Enforcement | Civil penalties, infringement notices, enforceable undertakings and court proceedings | AER and courts | Main national enforcement pathway for NER and NECF contraventions. |
| State regimes | State electricity, safety, planning and licensing legislation | State and territory regulators | Generation, network or retail licensing where retained; technical safety, planning and electrical installation controls. |

## Host laws and application model

| national pillar | South Australian host law | rules | rule maker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wholesale electricity and networks | National Electricity (South Australia) Act 1996, Schedule: National Electricity Law | National Electricity Rules | AEMC |
| Retail energy in NECF jurisdictions | National Energy Retail Law (South Australia) Act 2011, Schedule: National Energy Retail Law | National Energy Retail Rules | AEMC |
| Gas | National Gas (South Australia) Act 2008, Schedule: National Gas Law | National Gas Rules | AEMC |

Application Acts make the national law apply as law of the adopting jurisdiction. Check current legislation for local derogations and commencement differences before relying on a statement in this KB.

## NECF adoption map

| jurisdiction | NECF status | retail consequence |
|---|---|---|
| New South Wales | Adopted | AER retailer authorisation applies; NSW ombudsman membership remains required. |
| Queensland | Adopted | AER retailer authorisation applies, with local pricing and regional policy differences. |
| South Australia | Adopted | AER retailer authorisation applies; ESCOSA no longer issues ordinary retail licences. |
| Tasmania | Adopted | AER retailer authorisation applies, with Tasmanian retail-market features. |
| Australian Capital Territory | Adopted | AER retailer authorisation applies. |
| Victoria | Not adopted | Retailers need an ESC Victoria licence and must comply with the Victorian Energy Retail Code of Practice. |
| Western Australia | Not adopted | ERA licensing and WA energy retail legislation apply. |
| Northern Territory | Not adopted | Utilities Commission licensing and NT electricity legislation apply. |

An AER retailer authorisation is not an all-state retail licence. It covers NECF jurisdictions. Victoria, Western Australia and the Northern Territory require separate analysis.

## Rule changes and law changes

| reform type | pathway | key constraint |
|---|---|---|
| National law amendment | Ministerial policy decision followed by South Australian lead-legislation amendment and application across participating jurisdictions | Requires ministerial and parliamentary process. |
| Rule change | Request to AEMC under the relevant national law rule-making powers | AEMC must assess the rule against the National Electricity Objective or equivalent objective. |
| AEMO procedure change | AEMO consultation under the relevant rules | Must remain within the authorising rule and consultation requirements. |

Any person may request an AEMC rule change, although ministerial and market-body requests often carry practical priority. Standard rule changes include consultation and draft/final determination stages. Expedited and fast-track processes exist for defined categories such as urgent or non-controversial rules.

## Regulator roles

| body | role |
|---|---|
| AEMC | Makes and amends national energy rules and conducts market reviews. |
| AER | Enforces the NEL, NER, NERL and NERR; regulates electricity networks; grants retailer authorisations; publishes penalty indexation amounts and compliance priorities. |
| AEMO | Operates the NEM and WEM, manages power-system security, registration and dispatch, and administers rule-authorised procedures. |
| ACCC | Enforces Australian Consumer Law and competition law, including energy retail marketing and unsolicited sales conduct. |
| ACMA | Enforces the Do Not Call Register, telemarketing and spam frameworks. |
| State safety and economic regulators | Administer retained licensing, technical safety, electrical installation, distribution safety, retail code or pricing functions. |

## State and territory electricity regulators

| regulator | jurisdiction | core electricity functions |
|---|---|---|
| ESCOSA | South Australia | Generation, transmission and distribution licensing under SA law; retained electricity-industry economic regulation; retail authorisation is AER/NECF. |
| ESC Victoria | Victoria | Generation, transmission, distribution and retail licensing or exemptions; Victorian Energy Retail Code of Practice; Victorian Default Offer; penalty notices and court proceedings under Victorian legislation. |
| IPART | New South Wales | Network licence administration and compliance monitoring; NSW does not operate a general state generation-licence regime for NEM generators. |
| Under Treasurer, Queensland Treasury | Queensland | Electricity Act 1994 regulator for generation, transmission and distribution authorities and special approvals; authority holders submit annual compliance reports to the regulator. QCA has separate pricing, code and reporting functions. |
| OTTER | Tasmania | Electricity licensing and Tasmanian code/pricing functions. |
| ICRC | Australian Capital Territory | Utilities licensing and regulated retail price investigations under ACT legislation. |
| Utilities Commission | Northern Territory | Generation, network and retail licensing under NT electricity legislation. |
| ERA | Western Australia | Electricity licensing and WEM-related regulatory functions under WA legislation. |

## BESS and generation licensing snapshot

| jurisdiction | licensing implication for a grid-scale BESS |
|---|---|
| South Australia | AEMO registration is not enough. Check ESCOSA generation licensing and technical-regulator requirements. |
| New South Wales | No general state generation licence. Check AEMO registration, planning approval, connection and safety obligations. |
| Victoria | Generation usually requires an ESC licence unless an exemption applies. The General Exemption Order framework is important for smaller facilities. |
| Queensland | Check state generation authority requirements and exemptions under Queensland legislation. |
| Tasmania | Check OTTER licensing requirements. |
| ACT | Check Utilities Act and ICRC licensing guidance; generation may not be regulated in the same way as network/retail utilities. |
| Northern Territory | Check Utilities Commission generation licensing. |
| Western Australia | Check ERA licensing and WEM registration obligations. |

## Civil penalties and enforcement tools

The national energy penalty framework was materially strengthened from 29 January 2021 and indexed from 1 July 2023 and 1 July 2026. AER publishes the indexed civil and infringement amounts.

For body corporates from 1 July 2026:

| tier | maximum court penalty |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Greater of $12,390,000, three times attributable benefit, or 10% of annual turnover if benefit cannot be determined. |
| Tier 2 | $1,778,000 plus $89,000 for each day the breach continues. |
| Tier 3 | $210,600 plus $21,100 for each day the breach continues. |

For body corporate infringement notices from 1 July 2026:

| tier | infringement amount |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 or Tier 2 | $84,000 or a lesser prescribed amount. |
| Tier 3 | $42,000, or $8,410 where the AER makes the relevant determination, or a lesser prescribed amount. |

Always check whether the relevant provision is Tier 1, Tier 2 or Tier 3 in the current regulations before stating a maximum penalty.

## Coverage caveats

- State generation-licence statements are a routing guide, not a substitute for checking the current state Act, regulations, exemptions and licence decisions.
- Penalty amounts change through statutory indexation; this page uses AER's published 1 July 2026 values.
- This page does not cover all state safety, planning, environmental or work health and safety approvals.
