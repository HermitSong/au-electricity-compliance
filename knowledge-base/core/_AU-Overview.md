## Electricity Markets

Australia has two major organised wholesale electricity markets, but they do not cover every Australian electricity system. Retail-law boundaries also differ from wholesale-market boundaries.

### Jurisdiction Matrix

| Framework | Coverage | Exclusions or overlays |
|-----------|----------|------------------------|
| **NEM wholesale and power system** | QLD, NSW, VIC, SA and TAS; ACT participates through the NSW region | Excludes WA and NT |
| **NECF retail** | NSW, QLD, SA, TAS and ACT | Excludes Victoria, WA and NT |
| **Victorian retail** | Victoria under the ESC framework | Victoria remains a NEM wholesale jurisdiction |
| **WEM wholesale** | SWIS in Western Australia | Does not cover all WA systems; separate from NEM and NECF |
| **Northern Territory** | Separate NT electricity systems and legislation | Outside NEM, NECF and WEM |

### National Electricity Market (NEM)

- Covers Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia and Tasmania; the ACT participates through the NSW region
- Operated by [[Regulatory-Bodies#AEMO|AEMO]]
- 5-minute dispatch and settlement (since October 2021)
- Gross pool, mandatory dispatch market
- Price cap: $20,300/MWh (from 1 July 2025); floor: -$1,000/MWh
- See [[Market-Structure/NEM-Overview]]

### Wholesale Electricity Market (WEM)

- Covers the South West Interconnected System (SWIS) in Western Australia
- Capacity mechanism + energy market
- Undergoing significant reform (new flexibility product for BESS)
- See [[Market-Structure/WEM-Overview]]

### Retail Regimes

- The **NECF**, comprising NERL and NERR as applied in each participating jurisdiction, governs retail activity in NSW, QLD, SA, TAS and ACT and is administered and enforced by the [[Regulatory-Bodies#AER|AER]].
- **Victoria is in the NEM but outside the NECF**. The ESC administers the separate Victorian retail regime.
- **WA and NT are outside both the NEM and NECF** and require their own retail-law and licensing analysis.
- Price safety nets are the **DMO** in prescribed NSW, SA and south-east Queensland areas and the separate **VDO** in Victoria; see [[Market-Structure/Retail-Pricing-DMO-VDO]].
- The NECF compliance stack, including RoLR, life support, hardship, exempt selling and CDR, is summarised in [[Market-Structure/Retail-Compliance]].
- The [[Market-Structure/Retailer-Authorisation|AER Retailer Authorisation]] pathway applies only in NECF jurisdictions.

## Rooftop Solar

Australia has the world's highest rooftop solar penetration rate. By mid-2025, 4.2 million homes and businesses had installed 26.8 GW of rooftop solar capacity. This creates the "solar sponge" effect where daytime wholesale prices are regularly driven to zero or negative, reshaping market dynamics.

## Station Tiers

This knowledge base classifies Australian generation assets into four internal size tiers. These labels are navigation aids, not legal registration classes. AEMO classification depends on technology, capacity, dispatchability, exemptions and the applicable rules; for example, a battery at or above 5 MW follows the scheduled bidirectional unit pathway described in [[Market-Structure/Battery-5MW-Registration-Pathway]]. WA and NT require separate classification analysis.

### Large-Scale Generation (>30 MW)

Scheduled generators in the NEM. Includes coal-fired power stations, large wind farms, utility-scale solar farms, gas peakers, hydro, and grid-scale batteries. Subject to full [[Large-Scale-Generation/Grid-Requirements|Generator Performance Standards (GPS)]] and NER Chapter 5 connection.

- See [[Large-Scale-Generation/Engineering-Standards]]
- See [[Large-Scale-Generation/Market-Data]]

### Medium-Scale (5-30 MW)

Assets in this internal size tier may be embedded in distribution networks or connected at sub-transmission level. They are not automatically semi-scheduled: NEM registration and connection treatment must be determined from the resource type, exact capacity, exemption status and applicable NER pathway.

- See [[Medium-Scale/Engineering-Standards]]
- See [[Medium-Scale/Grid-Requirements]]

### Commercial-Industrial (100 kW - 5 MW)

Behind-the-meter systems for commercial and industrial premises. C&I solar, battery systems, and demand response assets. Connected under DNSP connection standards with export limits.

- See [[Commercial-Industrial/Engineering-Standards]]
- See [[Commercial-Industrial/Market-Data]]

### Residential (<100 kW)

Rooftop solar systems (typically 5-15 kW), home batteries (5-15 kWh), and emerging EV integration. Connected under AS/NZS 4777.2 inverter requirements.

- See [[Residential/Engineering-Standards]]
- See [[Residential/Market-Data]]

## Key Trends

1. **Coal Closure Acceleration** -- Coal generation at record lows; full exit projected by late 2030s-2040s depending on scenario
2. **Battery Storage Boom** -- Australia became world's third-largest utility BESS market in 2025; 4.9 GWh commissioned in 2025 alone
3. **REZ Development** -- 44 potential Renewable Energy Zones identified in Draft 2026 ISP
4. **Snowy 2.0** -- 2 GW / 350 GWh pumped hydro under construction
5. **Corporate PPA Growth** -- Sub-$40/MWh long-term contracts driving large-scale renewable investment
