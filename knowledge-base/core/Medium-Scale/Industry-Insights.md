# Industry Insights -- Medium-Scale Generation (5-30 MW)

## Renewable Energy Zones and Medium-Scale

While [[../Large-Scale-Generation/Industry-Insights#Renewable Energy Zone (REZ) Development|REZs]] are primarily designed for utility-scale projects, medium-scale generators can benefit from REZ infrastructure:

### Opportunities

| Opportunity | Detail |
|------------|--------|
| Shared transmission access | REZ access rights reduce connection costs |
| Community-scale participation | Some REZ frameworks allow sub-30 MW projects to connect within REZ |
| Local supply matching | Medium-scale projects serving local industrial loads within REZ catchments |
| REZ support infrastructure | Roads, communications, and workforce developed for REZ benefit all scales |

### Challenges

| Challenge | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Access right allocation | REZ access frameworks favour large-scale projects (higher capacity per connection) |
| Minimum lot size | Some REZ coordination arrangements may have minimum MW thresholds |
| Network charges | REZ connection charges may not scale well for smaller projects |

## Hybrid Projects

Hybrid projects combining multiple generation and storage technologies at a single site are a growing trend in the medium-scale segment.

### Common Hybrid Configurations

| Configuration | Typical Size | Advantages |
|--------------|-------------|------------|
| Solar + Battery | 5-20 MW solar + 2-10 MW / 4-20 MWh battery | Time-shift solar to peak; FCAS revenue |
| Wind + Solar | 10-30 MW combined | Complementary generation profiles |
| Wind + Battery | 5-20 MW wind + 2-10 MW battery | Firm wind output; reduce curtailment |
| Solar + Battery + Diesel | 5-15 MW | Remote/mini-grid; diesel backup |
| Solar + Agriculture (Agrivoltaics) | 5-20 MW | Dual land use; reduced land-use opposition |

### Hybrid Project Benefits

1. **Higher capacity factor** -- Combined wind + solar achieves 30-50% capacity factor vs 20-28% for solar alone
2. **Firming** -- Battery storage enables dispatchable output, increasing value
3. **Shared infrastructure** -- Single grid connection, shared substation and SCADA
4. **Revenue diversification** -- Access to energy, FCAS, and network support revenue streams
5. **Reduced curtailment** -- Battery absorbs excess generation during export-limited periods

### Regulatory Considerations for Hybrids

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| NEM registration | May register as single connection point with multiple units |
| Export limit management | Battery enables compliance with static export limits |
| AEMO classification | Hybrid systems can be classified as scheduled or semi-scheduled |
| Metering | Separate metering for each technology may be required for LGC purposes |

## Co-location Trends

Co-location refers to siting generation/storage at or near existing infrastructure.

### Common Co-location Scenarios

| Scenario | Example |
|----------|---------|
| Solar on industrial rooftop + ground-mount | Factory roof + adjacent paddock |
| Generation at retiring coal sites | Solar/battery on former coal station land |
| Storage at existing substations | BESS adjacent to DNSP substation |
| Generation at water treatment plants | Solar powering pumps + export surplus |
| Mining co-location | Solar + battery reducing diesel at mine sites |

### Co-location Advantages

- **Existing grid connection** -- Leverages existing connection capacity
- **Reduced development risk** -- Known site conditions, existing approvals base
- **Workforce transition** -- Former coal station workers retrained for new technology
- **Community acceptance** -- Industrial sites already zoned for energy use

## Emerging Trends

### Demand Response Aggregation

Medium-scale generators (especially batteries) are increasingly aggregated into demand response portfolios:
- [[../Regulatory-Bodies#AEMO|AEMO]] Wholesale Demand Response Mechanism (WDRM) enables demand-side participation in the NEM
- Aggregators combine multiple medium-scale BESS into virtual portfolios
- Revenue from avoided wholesale energy purchases and FCAS

### Community Energy

- Community-owned medium-scale solar farms (2-20 MW)
- CER-administered Community Batteries program
- [[../Regulatory-Bodies#ARENA|ARENA]]-funded community energy projects
- Local councils investing in medium-scale solar + storage

### Grid-Forming Inverters

- Medium-scale projects increasingly deploying grid-forming inverters
- Provides system strength contribution without synchronous machines
- Valued by DNSPs for weak grid areas
- Potential for premium network support payments
