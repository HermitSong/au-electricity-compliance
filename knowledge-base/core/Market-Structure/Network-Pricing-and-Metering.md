# Australian Network Pricing, Metering and DNSP Directory

> Verified to August 2026. The NER and AER material on this page applies to the NEM jurisdictions. Western Australia and the Northern Territory operate separate network and regulatory frameworks and are listed only to prevent accidental cross-jurisdiction use.

## 1. Network Pricing: DUoS, TUoS and TSS

- A distribution network charge commonly combines **distribution use of system (DUoS)**, which recovers regulated DNSP costs; a pass-through component for **transmission use of system (TUoS)**; metering charges; and applicable government or jurisdictional scheme charges.
- Under **NER Chapter 6**, each NEM DNSP submits a **Tariff Structure Statement (TSS)** to the AER for approval at the start of each five-year regulatory control period. Its annual pricing proposal must then comply with the approved TSS.
- Service classifications include standard control, alternative control, negotiated and unregulated services.
- Connection pathways distinguish basic or standard connection offers from negotiated connection contracts under NER Chapters 5 and 5A.
- AER network tariff reform promotes cost-reflective structures, including time-of-use and demand tariffs. The **2021 DER access and pricing reforms** also enabled two-way or export tariff structures.

## 2. Metering and Power of Choice

- **Power of Choice metering competition** commenced on **1 December 2017** in ACT, NSW, QLD, SA and TAS. **Victoria is excluded from that rollout model**, because Victorian distributors retain the relevant installation role.
- The **Metering Coordinator (MC)** replaced the former Responsible Person role and arranges metering for each connection point.
- The AEMC published the final report of its **Review of the Regulatory Framework for Metering Services** in August 2023, containing 21 recommendations.
- The final **Accelerating Smart Meter Deployment** rule was made on **28 November 2024**, with a policy objective of universal smart-meter deployment by 2030.

## 3. Distribution Network Operators and Jurisdiction Boundaries

| Jurisdiction | Distribution network operator | Market framework |
|--------------|-------------------------------|------------------|
| NSW | Ausgrid, Endeavour Energy, Essential Energy | NEM |
| QLD | Energex, Ergon Energy | NEM, with separate treatment required for isolated networks |
| SA | **SA Power Networks (SAPN)**, which has the deepest coverage in this knowledge base | NEM |
| VIC | Powercor, CitiPower, United Energy, AusNet Services, Jemena | NEM wholesale and network framework; separate Victorian retail regime |
| TAS | TasNetworks | NEM |
| ACT | Evoenergy | NEM through the NSW region; NECF retail jurisdiction |
| WA | Western Power | **SWIS/WEM; not NEM** |
| NT | Power and Water | **Separate NT framework; not NEM, NECF or WEM** |

> Each distributor publishes its own embedded-generation connection standard and SCADA requirements. The reviewed material identifies SAPN's Modbus/TS133-TS134 architecture, frequent use of DNP3 elsewhere in the NEM, an IEC 61850 orientation in the reviewed AusNet material, and API or cloud patterns in the reviewed WA DER material. These are not universal mandates. See [[DNSP-Connection-Standards]] for the provider-by-provider comparison and confidence qualifications.
