# AER Retailer Authorisation — Detailed Process

> **Jurisdiction boundary:** This AER authorisation pathway applies to the NECF jurisdictions: NSW, QLD, SA, TAS and ACT. Victoria is a NEM wholesale jurisdiction but uses the separate ESC retail licensing framework. WA and NT are outside both the NEM and NECF and require their own retail-law analysis.

## Regulatory Framework

### National Energy Retail Law (NERL) Jurisdictions

| State/Territory | Adopts NERL? | Authority |
|----------------|-------------|-----------|
| NSW | ✅ Yes | AER (national) |
| QLD | ✅ Yes | AER (national) |
| SA | ✅ Yes | AER (national) |
| TAS | ✅ Yes | AER (national) |
| ACT | ✅ Yes | AER (national); ICRC retains price regulation |
| **VIC** | ❌ No | ESC (Essential Services Commission) — separate Victorian framework |
| **WA** | ❌ No | ERA (Economic Regulation Authority) — separate WEM framework |
| **NT** | ❌ No | NT Utilities Commission — Electricity Reform Act 2000 |

- **[AER Retailer Authorisation Guideline v3 (July 2024)](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/2024-07/AER%20-%20Retailer%20authorisation%20guideline%20-%20July%202024.pdf)** — Main guidance
- **[AER Retail Exempt Selling Guideline v7 (Aug 2025)](https://www.aer.gov.au/industry/registers/resources/guidelines/retail-exempt-selling-guideline)** — Embedded networks

- **National Energy Retail Rules (NERR)** — AEMC-made rules

## Three Entry Criteria

AER must be satisfied on ALL three before granting authorisation:

### Criterion 1: Organisational and Technical Capacity

**What AER Assesses:**

| Element | Evidence Required |
|---------|------------------|
| Management team | CVs showing energy retail experience |
| Business procedures | Documented operational procedures |
| Customer service | Helpdesk capability, staffing model, SLAs |
| Billing systems | IT system specifications, vendor agreements |
| Hardship handling | Draft hardship policy with detailed procedures |
| Complaint resolution | Formal complaint process (escalation, EWOQ/EWON referral) |
| IT systems | MSATS connectivity, settlement integration, CRM |
| Data management | Privacy compliance, security, retention |

### Criterion 2: Financial Capacity & Viability

**Financial Documentation Required:**

```
3-Year Financial Projections (mandatory):
├── Profit & Loss
│   ├── Year 1: Monthly detail
│   ├── Year 2: Quarterly detail
│   └── Year 3: Annual detail
├── Cash Flow
│   └── Monthly detail throughout (critical for working capital assessment)
├── Balance Sheet
│   └── Year-end positions
└── Sensitivity Analysis
    ├── Customer acquisition variations (±20%, ±50%)
    ├── Wholesale price variations (P50, P75, P90 scenarios)
    ├── Margin compression scenarios
    └── Regulatory cost increases
```

**Funding Evidence:**
- Bank facility letters
- Investor commitment letters
- Capital adequacy statements
- Working capital requirements analysis
- Financial assumptions methodology

### Criterion 3: Suitability (Fit & Proper Test)

**Per Director / Senior Executive:**

- Statutory character declaration
- National Police Clearance
- Bankruptcy/insolvency history disclosure
- Professional references (industry peers, former regulators)
- Disclosure of any:
  - Indictable criminal convictions
  - Previous retail licence cancellations
  - Consumer protection law breaches
  - Director disqualifications

## Application Process — Detailed Timeline

### Phase 1: Pre-Submission Preparation (3-6 months)

**Activities:**
1. Develop business plan (15-30 pages)
2. Hire/contract experienced retail management team
3. Develop billing system specifications
4. Draft hardship policy
5. Prepare retail contract templates
6. Build customer service procedures
7. Establish IT systems (or vendor contracts)
8. Compile financial projections with sensitivity analysis
9. Conduct director background checks
10. Engage legal advisors for application review

### Phase 2: AEMO Market Customer Registration (Parallel, 1-2 months)

Required to enable wholesale market purchases. See [[Market-Participants-Licensing#1.2 Market Customer]] for details.

### Phase 3: AER Application Submission

**Submission:**
- Email to: **aerauthorisations@aer.gov.au**
- All documents signed and certified
- Include application fee (varies)

**Application Package:**

| Section | Contents |
|---------|----------|
| **Forms** | Application form, checklist, declaration template |
| **Business Plan** | 3-5 year strategy, market positioning, growth plan |
| **Operational Documents** | Org chart, staffing plan, procedures |
| **Customer-Facing Documents** | Contract templates, hardship policy, complaints procedure |
| **Financial Documents** | 3-year P&L, cash flow, balance sheet, sensitivity analysis |
| **Funding Evidence** | Facility letters, investor commitments |
| **Director Information** | CVs, character declarations, police clearances |
| **Technical Capability** | IT system specs, MSATS connectivity, billing system |

### Phase 4: AER Assessment (12-24 weeks)

```
Week 1-4:   Completeness Review
            └── AER may request clarifications
            └── Applicant to provide within reasonable timeframe

Week 4-10:  Detailed Assessment
            ├── Three-criteria evaluation
            ├── Possible meetings with applicant
            ├── Financial projection scrutiny
            └── Reference checks on directors

Week 10-14: Public Consultation
            ├── Application posted on AER website
            ├── 4-week public submission period
            └── Applicant may respond to submissions

Week 14-20: Final Determination
            ├── Approve unconditionally, OR
            ├── Approve with conditions, OR
            └── Refuse
```

### Phase 5: Conditional Compliance (if applicable, +3 months max)

If AER grants conditional approval:
- **Strict deadline: 3 months** to satisfy conditions
- **Non-compliance** = application **deemed refused**
- Common conditions:
  - Provide additional financial guarantees
  - Update hardship policy specifics
  - Strengthen IT system before launch
  - Appoint specific senior personnel

### Phase 6: Post-Authorisation Compliance

| Obligation | Frequency |
|-----------|-----------|
| NERR compliance | Continuous |
| Hardship policy implementation | Continuous |
| Settlement payments | Weekly billing; **SSC 9-business-day cycle from 9 Aug 2026** (see [[Settlement-Prudential]]) |
| Annual compliance report | Annual |
| Customer information updates | Per change |
| Regulatory inspections | Ad hoc |
| Pricing transparency | Continuous |

## Default Market Offer (DMO)

### What is DMO?

AER-set **maximum price** for standing offer contracts to small customers in regulated regions.

### Application

| Region | DMO Applies? |
|--------|--------------|
| NSW | ✅ Yes |
| South East Queensland | ✅ Yes |
| South Australia | ✅ Yes |
| Other regions | ❌ No (market offers only or different regulation) |

### Retailer Obligations

- **Cannot** charge above DMO for standing offers
- **May** offer below-DMO rates as market offers
- **Must** display DMO prominently in marketing
- **Must** ensure billing transparency
- **Reviewed annually** by AER

### Current DMO

- **DMO 8 — DMO 2026-27** (current determination; final ~26 May 2026; effective **1 Jul 2026 – 30 Jun 2027**)
- Full values + Victorian Default Offer (VDO) → see [[Retail-Pricing-DMO-VDO]]
- Published on AER website with historical versions

## Retailer Reliability Obligation (RRO)

### Purpose

Reliability framework requiring retailers and major users to contract for sufficient capacity during system peak demand. Designed in response to renewable energy growth.

### Triggering Mechanism

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Trigger source | AEMO identifies a "reliability gap" in forecasts |
| Notice period | T-3 years (3 years ahead) and T-1 (1 year ahead) |
| Most recent trigger | South Australia (recent years) |

### Liable Entities

- **Market Customers with annual consumption >10 GWh**
- Includes:
  - Retailers (aggregated customer load)
  - Major industrial users (direct purchasers)

### Obligations When Triggered

1. Demonstrate sufficient supply/demand-side contracts to meet share of expected peak demand
2. Contract for "firming" capacity (generation or demand response)
3. Submit "Voluntary Book Build" or "Mandatory Net Contract Position"
4. Report to AEMO on compliance
5. Penalties for non-compliance (financial)

### Alternative Compliance

- Contribute to AEMO administered reserve scheme
- Use existing PPAs and contracts (must demonstrate sufficient firming)

**Reference:** [DCCEEW RRO Page](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/energy/programs/retailer-reliability-obligation)

## Hardship Policy Requirements

All authorised retailers must develop, maintain, and implement a hardship policy covering:

### 1. Identification of Hardship Customers

- Customers unable to pay bills due to financial hardship
- Recognition of family violence as hardship indicator
- Proactive identification mechanisms (high arrears, payment plan failures)

### 2. Hardship Support Measures

| Measure | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Payment plan flexibility | Extended timeframes, reduced amounts |
| Late payment fee waivers | In hardship circumstances |
| Centrepay eligibility | Government direct-debit option support |
| Energy efficiency advice | Guidance to reduce consumption |
| Emergency relief referrals | Connection to community support |

### 3. Ongoing Support

- Regular contact and review of payment plans
- Assistance programs and referrals
- **No disconnection** during hardship negotiations (with exceptions per NERR)
- Protections for vulnerable customers

### Regulatory Oversight

- Hardship policies subject to **AER approval**
- Compliance with **AER Customer Hardship Policy Guideline (April 2019)**
- AER monitoring of retailer hardship compliance
- Annual compliance reporting

## AER Retail Exempt Selling — Embedded Networks

### Overview

For embedded networks (apartments, shopping centres, caravan parks, retirement villages, industrial parks):

### Three Exemption Categories

| Category | Application Required | Status |
|----------|---------------------|--------|
| **Deemed Exemption** | No application historically | **CLOSED** to new networks (v7, Aug 2025) |
| **Negotiated Exemption** | Yes — registration | Open |
| **Approved Exemption** | Yes — case-by-case approval | Open |

### Critical Change: v7 (August 2025)

**Deemed exemptions CLOSED to new embedded network configurations.** All new networks must:
1. Register through Negotiated or Approved pathway
2. Demonstrate compliance with consumer protection requirements
3. Submit to AER scrutiny

**Existing networks:** Grandfathered but subject to increased oversight.

### Key Compliance Obligations for Exempt Sellers

- Customer contract transparency
- Billing dispute procedures
- Hardship support (where applicable)
- AER notification of complaints exceeding thresholds
- Metering standards compliance
- Consumer protection compliance (similar to NERR)

## Embedded Network Manager (ENM)

### When ENM Required

**Mandatory** when:
- Small customer in embedded network accepts a market retail offer
- Customer's cooling-off period (5 business days) has expired

### AEMO ENM Accreditation

ENM is accredited by AEMO (separate from AER exempt seller registration).

**Application Documents:**
- ENM Accreditation application form
- Service delivery capability statement
- Technical specs for metering and settlement systems
- Financial viability evidence
- Business continuity / disaster recovery plans
- Customer service procedures
- MSATS system capability documentation

### Ongoing Obligations

- Register NMI (National Metering Identifier) for each customer
- Register Child NMIs in MSATS (Market Settlement and Transfer Solutions)
- Maintain NMI Standing Data
- Assign Distribution Loss Factors (DLF)
- Comply with ENM Service Level Procedure
- Coordinate with Metering Provider and Metering Coordinator

### Reference Documents

- [AEMO Accreditation & Registration](https://www.aemo.com.au/energy-systems/electricity/national-electricity-market-nem/market-operations/retail-and-metering/accreditation-and-registration)
- [Guide to Embedded Networks (PDF)](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/electricity/nem/retail_and_metering/accreditation/guide-to-embedded-networks.pdf)
- [AER Embedded Network Manager Page](https://www.aer.gov.au/embedded-network-manager)

## State-Specific Variations

### Victoria (ESC)

- **Authority:** Essential Services Commission
- **Framework:** Victorian Energy Retail Code of Practice
- **Apply:** [ESC Apply for Licence](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/electricity-and-gas/licences-exemptions-and-trial-waivers/electricity-and-gas-licences/apply-electricity-or-gas-licence)
- **Timeline:** 8-10 weeks once complete
- **2024 Change:** July 2024 amendment — wholesale-only sales no longer require retail licence (s.16(1) of Electricity Industry Act 2000)

### Western Australia (ERA)

- **Authority:** Economic Regulation Authority (separate WEM)
- **Customer Classes:**
  - Non-contestable: <50 MWh/year (Synergy regulated supply)
  - Contestable: 50-160 MWh/year
  - Large: >160 MWh/year
- **Financial Assessment Cost:** ~$5,000
- **Reference:** [ERA Wholesale Electricity Market](https://www.erawa.com.au/electricity/wholesale-electricity-market)

### Northern Territory (Utilities Commission)

- **Authority:** NT Utilities Commission
- **Framework:** Electricity Reform Act 2000
- **Status:** Full retail contestability available
- **Compliance:** Electricity Retail Supply Code
- **Reference:** [NT Licensing](https://utilicom.nt.gov.au/electricity/licensing)

### ACT (ICRC + AER)

- ICRC NO LONGER licenses retailers (uses AER national process)
- ICRC retains: Standing offer price regulation (ActewAGL only)
- ACT Retail Electricity (Transparency and Comparability) Code v4.0 (Nov 2024)
- **Reference:** [ICRC Energy](https://www.icrc.act.gov.au/energy/electricity)
