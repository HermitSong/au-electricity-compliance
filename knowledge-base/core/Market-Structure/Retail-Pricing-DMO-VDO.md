# Australian Retail Price Safety Nets: DMO and VDO, 2026-27

> Verified to August 2026. The DMO and VDO are separate default-price regimes. Each acts as a maximum price for the applicable standing-offer class and as a reference point for specified customer information. **The AER DMO/Better Bills framework must not be treated as the governing Victorian instrument; Victoria applies the ESC VDO and separate Victorian retail requirements.** Neither regime applies generally across WA or NT.

## 1. Default Market Offer: AER

- **Current instrument:** DMO 2026-27, also called **DMO 8**. The final determination was published around **26 May 2026** and applies from **1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027**.
- **Legal basis:** *Competition and Consumer (Industry Code - Electricity Retail) Regulations 2019*. The AER uses a bottom-up assessment of efficient costs.
- **Coverage:** five distribution zones across three jurisdictions: **NSW** (Ausgrid, Endeavour Energy and Essential Energy), **south-east Queensland** (Energex), and **South Australia** (SA Power Networks). It excludes **Victoria**, which has the VDO; **regional Queensland** in the Ergon area, where Queensland Government pricing applies; and embedded networks unless a specific rule provides otherwise.

**Approximate DMO 8 annual residential flat-rate caps, including GST:**

| Distribution zone | DMO 8 annual cap | Year-on-year change |
|-------------------|------------------|---------------------|
| NSW - Ausgrid | ~$1,899 | -3.4% |
| NSW - Endeavour Energy | ~$2,328 | -3.4% |
| NSW - Essential Energy | ~$2,604 | -5.0% |
| South-east Queensland - Energex | ~$1,988 | -7.2% |
| South Australia - SA Power Networks | ~$2,334 | **+1.4%** |

- Residential **time-of-use** amounts decreased across the recorded zones, from -1.1% in SA to -10.7% in south-east Queensland; NSW decreases ranged from -3.7% to -7.7%.
- **Small-business** decreases were larger: SA -6.8% to -12.1%, south-east Queensland -10.4% to -14.0%, and NSW -9.0% to -20.9%.

> DMO 8 reduced the recorded amount for most customer categories. The SA residential flat-rate amount was the exception, increasing by 1.4%.

## 2. Victorian Default Offer: ESC

- **Current instrument:** VDO 2026-27. The final decision was issued on **25 May 2026** and took effect on **1 July 2026**.
- The recorded Victorian residential average, based on 4,000 kWh per year, is approximately **$1,591**, a reduction of **$84 or 5%** year on year. Amounts vary by distribution zone.
- The **Essential Services Commission (ESC)** sets the VDO under the separate Victorian retail regime. Victoria is a NEM wholesale jurisdiction but is not an NECF/DMO retail jurisdiction.

## 3. Billing and Better-Offer Obligations

- **AER Better Bills Guideline v2, dated 30 January 2023**, applies within the AER/NECF framework and requires prescribed bill information, including relevant reference-price comparisons and better-offer messaging where the criteria are met.
- Victoria has separate ESC retail-code and billing requirements. The AER Better Bills Guideline does not become a Victorian obligation merely because the VDO is also a reference price. This page does not identify a current ESC billing instrument beyond the linked VDO decision; verify the current ESC code or guideline before relying on a Victorian bill design.

## 4. Time-Sensitive Notes

- DMO and VDO determinations are generally made in May for commencement on 1 July. DMO 9 and VDO 2027-28 would ordinarily be expected around May 2027, but publication dates must be confirmed rather than assumed.
- The values and percentages above are verified approximations. Use the exact amount for each distribution zone and tariff type in the [AER final determination](https://www.aer.gov.au/industry/registers/resources/reviews/default-market-offer-2026-27) or [ESC VDO final decision](https://www.esc.vic.gov.au/electricity-and-gas/prices-tariffs-and-benchmarks/victorian-default-offer/victorian-default-offer-price-review-2026-27).
