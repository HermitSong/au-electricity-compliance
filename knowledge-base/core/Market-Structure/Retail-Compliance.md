# Australian Electricity Retail Compliance Framework

> Verified to August 2026. The **National Energy Customer Framework (NECF)** comprises the National Energy Retail Law (NERL), National Energy Retail Rules (NERR) and jurisdictional application legislation. It is administered and enforced by the AER in **NSW, QLD, SA, TAS and the ACT**. **Victoria is in the NEM but has not adopted the NECF** and applies the Essential Services Commission (ESC) retail framework, including the Energy Retail Code of Practice. **WA and NT are outside both the NEM and the NECF** and apply their own legislation. Do not treat a NEM wholesale obligation as proof that the NECF applies to retail activity in the same place.

## 2. Victoria: Separate ESC Retail Regime

Victoria does not apply the NECF. The **ESC Energy Retail Code of Practice** provides a separate retail compliance framework, including:

- The **Payment Difficulty Framework**, in Part 6 of the Code of Practice, effective from **1 January 2019**, together with the ESC Payment Difficulty Framework Guideline dated July 2024.
- The **Victorian Default Offer (VDO)**; see [[Retail-Pricing-DMO-VDO]].
- Parallel Victorian requirements for life-support customers, disconnection and related customer protections.

An AER NECF guideline is not, by itself, the governing Victorian retail instrument. Bind a Victorian conclusion to the current ESC code, guideline, decision or licence condition.

## 3. Western Australia and Northern Territory

- **Western Australia:** outside the NEM and NECF. Retail licensing is governed under the *Electricity Industry Act 2004* and administered by the Economic Regulation Authority (ERA); WEM rules govern the SWIS wholesale market. Confirm whether a project is in the SWIS or another WA system.
- **Northern Territory:** outside the NEM, NECF and WEM. Retail and utility regulation is administered under the separate NT framework, including the Utilities Commission. Territory-specific legislation and licence conditions must be checked directly.

## 4. Compliance Control Points

- **Authorisation is the entry gate in NECF jurisdictions:** an AER-authorised retailer must comply with the applicable NERL, NERR, authorisation conditions and current AER instruments.
- **Life support and hardship are recurring enforcement areas:** written policies are insufficient unless operational systems, staff actions and evidence match them. The A$12 million Origin life-support matter is a prominent example.
- **Embedded-network resale may use the exempt-selling framework:** the pathway and applicable exemption class must be verified under Guideline v7. It is not interchangeable with full retailer authorisation.
- **DMO and VDO are separate price-safety-net regimes:** DMO applies only in its prescribed AER areas, while VDO is Victorian. Marketing and bill-comparison duties must be tied to the correct jurisdictional instrument.
