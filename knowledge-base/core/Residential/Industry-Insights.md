# Industry Insights -- Residential (<100 kW)

## World-Leading Rooftop Solar Adoption

### Key Statistics

| Metric | Value | Date |
|--------|-------|------|
| Total rooftop solar capacity | 26.8 GW | Mid-2025 |
| Number of installations | 4.2 million homes and businesses | Mid-2025 |
| Household penetration rate | ~37% of detached houses | 2025 |
| Average new system size | 8-9 kW (trending up from 6.6 kW) | 2025 |
| Annual installations | ~350,000+ systems per year | 2024-2025 |
| Global ranking | #1 per capita rooftop solar | 2025 |

### Adoption by State

| State | Penetration Rate | Key Driver |
|-------|-----------------|-----------|
| SA | ~45% | Highest retail prices; best solar resource |
| QLD | ~40% | Strong solar resource; high retail prices |
| WA | ~38% | Good solar resource; Synergy tariff structure |
| NSW | ~35% | Largest market by volume |
| VIC | ~30% | Government rebate programs; growing rapidly |
| TAS | ~20% | Lower solar resource; lower retail prices |

### Growth Drivers

1. High retail electricity prices (30-45 c/kWh)
2. Rapid payback (3-5 years for solar-only)
3. STC rebates reducing upfront cost by $2,500-3,500
4. State rebate programs (VIC Solar Homes, SA Home Battery Scheme)
5. Strong installer competition driving down prices
6. Consumer awareness and neighbour effect

## Battery Uptake

### Current Market

| Metric | Value | Date |
|--------|-------|------|
| Residential batteries installed | ~400,000+ systems | End 2025 |
| Quarterly installations | ~30,000+ per quarter | Q4 2025 |
| Most popular capacity | 10-13.5 kWh | 2025 |
| Average installed cost | $8,000-$14,000 (after rebates) | 2025 |
| Leading brands | Tesla Powerwall 3, BYD, Enphase, SolarEdge | 2025 |

### Battery Adoption Drivers

1. **Declining FiT rates** -- Low export value makes self-consumption more valuable
2. **Time-of-use tariffs** -- Batteries shift consumption from peak to off-peak
3. **VPP programs** -- Additional revenue from aggregated dispatch
4. **Blackout protection** -- Backup power during grid outages (bushfire/storm)
5. **State rebates** -- VIC up to $2,950; SA Home Battery Scheme
6. **Cost reduction** -- Battery prices declining ~10-15% per year

### Battery Market Forecast

The residential battery market is expected to continue strong growth:
- Q4 2025 was a landmark period for household energy storage
- Pairing rate (battery + solar) increasing from ~15% to ~25% of new installations
- LFP chemistry dominating due to safety, longevity, and cost
- Whole-of-home backup systems becoming standard configuration

## EV Integration

### Current State

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| EV sales share (new vehicles) | ~10-12% (2025) |
| Total EVs on road | ~300,000+ |
| Average EV battery size | 60-80 kWh |
| Home charging prevalence | ~80% of EV charging occurs at home |

### Solar-EV Synergy

| Scenario | Annual Savings |
|----------|---------------|
| EV charged from grid (off-peak) | ~$600-900/year vs petrol |
| EV charged from rooftop solar | ~$1,200-1,800/year vs petrol |
| EV + solar + home battery | Maximised self-consumption; $1,500-2,500/year total savings |

### Vehicle-to-Home (V2H) and Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G)

| Technology | Status in Australia |
|-----------|-------------------|
| V2H (Vehicle-to-Home) | Emerging; supported by some EVs (Nissan Leaf, BYD Atto 3) |
| V2G (Vehicle-to-Grid) | Pilot programs; ARENA-funded trials |
| Bidirectional chargers | Limited availability; CHAdeMO and CCS2 capable |
| Regulatory framework | AEMO developing V2G integration rules |

### Smart Charging

- Time-of-use tariff optimisation (charge during off-peak/solar hours)
- Solar diversion: excess solar charges EV before export
- [[Market-Data#VPP Programs|VPP integration]]: EV battery available for grid services
- Load management: prevent EV charging from triggering demand peaks

## Solar Sponge Effect

### Description

The "solar sponge" or "duck curve" effect describes the mismatch between solar generation and electricity demand:

```
Demand Profile (typical summer day):

   MW
    |        ___
    |       /   \
    |      /     \___
    |  ___/          \___
    | /                  \
    |/ Solar Generation   \_____
    |     ____
    |    /    \
    |   /      \
    |  /  Net   \
    | /  Demand  \
    +-------------------> Time
      6am  12pm  6pm  12am
```

### Market Impact

| Effect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Negative daytime prices | NEM spot prices regularly go negative during midday in SA, VIC, QLD |
| Minimum demand records | NEM minimum operational demand falling each year as solar grows |
| Evening ramp | Steep demand ramp from ~3pm to 7pm as solar drops and demand peaks |
| System security | Low daytime demand reduces synchronous generation online, lowering system inertia and fault levels |

### Mitigation Strategies

| Strategy | Status |
|----------|--------|
| Home batteries | Growing rapidly; shift solar to evening use |
| EVs as flexible load | Charge during solar surplus hours |
| Hot water diversion | CER-supported "solar soak" programs; heat water during solar hours |
| Time-of-use tariffs | Incentivise consumption during solar surplus |
| Dynamic export | Allow higher exports when network can absorb; curtail when constrained |
| Community batteries | DNSP-deployed batteries on constrained feeders |
| Demand response | Shift flexible loads to solar hours |

### AEMO Response

AEMO has implemented several measures to manage high distributed solar:
- **DER visibility** -- DER Register providing real-time visibility of rooftop solar fleet
- **Emergency backstop** -- Ability to curtail DER in extreme system security events (SA first, now NEM-wide)
- **Minimum system load standard** -- New NER provisions for managing very low demand periods
- **Aggregate Forecasting** -- Improved forecasting of rooftop solar output for dispatch

## Future Outlook

1. **System sizes increasing** -- Average new system moving from 6.6 kW toward 10-13 kW
2. **Battery standard** -- Batteries becoming standard with new solar installations
3. **EV integration** -- Solar + battery + EV as integrated home energy system
4. **Virtual Power Plants** -- Millions of homes providing grid services through aggregation
5. **Electrification** -- Heat pumps, induction cooking, EV replacing gas and petrol
6. **Smart homes** -- AI-driven energy management optimising solar, battery, EV, and flexible loads
