# Industry Insights -- Large-Scale Generation

## Integrated System Plan (ISP)

### 2024 ISP (Published June 2024)

The ISP is [[Regulatory-Bodies#AEMO|AEMO's]] roadmap for the NEM's transmission and generation development over a 20+ year horizon.

Key findings from the 2024 ISP (Step Change scenario):
- Coal to fully exit the NEM by 2037-38
- 122 GW of new renewable capacity needed by 2050
- 61 GW of utility-scale solar, 47 GW of wind
- 46 GW / 640 GWh of storage (batteries + pumped hydro)
- $121 billion in total generation and storage investment to 2050
- 10,000 km of new transmission lines

### Draft 2026 ISP (Published 2025)

Updated projections reflecting policy changes and market developments:
- Coal exit timeline extended to 2048-49 under Step Change (reflecting QLD Energy Roadmap and slower NSW/VIC closures)
- However, AEMO notes coal may close faster than forecast due to rising costs, unplanned outages, and renewable competition
- 44 potential REZs identified (including 5 offshore wind zones)
- Increased storage requirements

## Renewable Energy Zone (REZ) Development

### ISP-Identified REZs by State

| State | Number of REZs | Key REZs |
|-------|---------------|----------|
| NSW | 5 declared | Central-West Orana (first to construct, 6 GW), New England, South-West, Hunter-Central Coast, Illawarra |
| QLD | 12 planned | Western Downs, Southern Downs, Far North QLD (in-flight); additional phases through 2030s |
| VIC | 6 proposed | Western Victoria, Murray River, Gippsland, South-West Victoria (final orders expected mid-2026) |
| SA | Multiple | Leigh Creek, Roxby Downs, Mid-North |
| TAS | 1+ | North-West Tasmania (linked to Marinus Link) |

### REZ Progress

- **Central-West Orana REZ (NSW)** -- Australia's first REZ; construction began late 2024; 4.5 GW network capacity by 2028, 6 GW ultimate; $20 billion in investment
- **EnergyCo NSW** coordinates NSW REZ delivery
- **VicGrid** coordinates Victorian REZ planning
- REZs are coordinated access regimes with shared transmission infrastructure, reducing connection costs for generators

## Battery Storage Boom

### 2025 Milestones

- 1.9 GW / 4.9 GWh of grid-scale BESS commissioned in 2025
- This single year matched the entire 2017-2024 cumulative total
- NEM operational BESS capacity doubled from 2 GW to 4.6 GW during 2025
- Australia became world's third-largest utility BESS market (behind China and US)
- Over A$2.4 billion invested in large-scale BESS in early 2025

### Key Drivers of Coal Exit

1. Rising maintenance costs and declining reliability
2. Renewable energy cost competitiveness (sub-$40/MWh PPAs)
3. Carbon policy (Safeguard Mechanism tightening)
4. Battery storage replacing peaking and firming roles
5. Worker transition support programs (e.g., Hunter region transition)

## Corporate PPA Market

- Long-term PPAs (10-15 years) are the primary investment signal for new large-scale renewables
- Contract prices sub-A$40/MWh for utility-scale solar and wind
- Aggregated PPAs enabling mid-tier firms to access utility-scale economics
- Major corporate buyers: miners, data centres, food processing, retail chains
- Contract sizes: 50-650 MW common for single corporate off-takers
