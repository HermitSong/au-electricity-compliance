# D04 - AER Retail Enforcement Case Library under the NECF

This page is a selected official case library for retail electricity compliance. It focuses on AER and Federal Court records under the National Energy Retail Law (NERL) and National Energy Retail Rules (NERR). Victorian ESC cases are included only where they help control design; they are not NERR authorities.

This page does not claim exhaustive coverage. For a chronological cross-domain index, see [D00 - Australian Electricity Enforcement Event Timeline, 2006-2026](D00-Enforcement-Event-Timeline-2006-2026.md).

## Priority risk map

| risk area | typical legal source | control implication |
|---|---|---|
| Hardship | NERL s 43 and NERR payment-plan rules | Hardship policy must be maintained, implemented and protected from automation failures. |
| Life support | NERR life support rules | Retailer systems must reconcile customer notices, registers, distributor B2B notifications and deregistration. |
| Explicit informed consent | NERL ss 38-39 | Sales, transfer, contract and VPP enrolment flows need consent evidence and customer identity checks. |
| Disconnection / de-energisation | NERR disconnection and de-energisation preconditions | Hardship, payment-plan, life support, notice and identity gates must be checked before service orders. |
| Centrepay / overcharging | NERR r 31 and NERL compliance-system obligations | Closed-account and third-party-payment reconciliation remains critical, but AGL Full Court limits the r 31 "overcharged" theory. |
| Better Bills | NERR r 25(1) and AER Better Bills Guideline v2 | Bills must meet content, better-offer and plan-identification requirements; current records are review/guidance rather than a named high-value AER penalty case. |
| Ombudsman membership | NERL s 86 and exemption conditions | Retailers, distributors and many exempt sellers must join the relevant scheme. |

### AGL Centrepay: Linked Research Chronology

Research review dated 6 September 2026, not operational legal clearance. The [case chain](../data/reviewed-case-chains/centrepay-chain.json) and pinpoint review (local-only artifact; not distributed) distinguish institution on 16 December 2022, liability reasons on 23 August 2024, relief on 19 December 2024 and appeal on 19 August 2026. `case_lookup.py` returns this chain with each associated canonical event.

Orders 2-5 of [2026] FCAFC 106 allow the appeal, dismiss the cross-appeal and replace all December orders with dismissal of the original proceeding. The historical $25 million and compliance-program orders are not surviving relief. The case does not establish permission to keep customer funds or a general exemption from compliance monitoring. The rule 31 question concerns an assertion of entitlement to an excessive payment, not receipt alone. See appellate reasons [125], [152]-[158] and [181]-[183].

Keep the later docket observations separate: the reviewed chain records filings on 26 August and 2 September 2026, with their contents and legal effect unverified. It also flags source-specific date discrepancies and a difference between the judgment's reproduction of rule 31(7) and the inspected AEMC compilations. Do not silently resolve these discrepancies or claim that no further appeal exists.

## Control patterns

| failure pattern | official examples | control design |
|---|---|---|
| Automation removed customers from hardship protections | Origin 2022; Red Energy 2025 | Hardship and payment-plan status should survive missed instalments, moves, account rebuilds and billing-system migrations unless compliant review gates are passed. |
| Life support data did not reach all required systems | EnergyAustralia 2022; Origin subsidiaries 2024; Powershop 2025 | Reconcile customer notices, retailer registers, distributor B2B notifications, information packs, deregistration and de-energisation blocks. |
| Sales or transfer flows lacked EIC evidence | EnergyAustralia 2015 and 2026; Simply Energy; Red; Alinta | Store consent recordings or digital artefacts, check identity and prevent transfers from address-only or third-party account errors. |
| Disconnection gates failed | AGL 2015; Origin 2017; EnergyAustralia 2026; Victorian WDP cases | Check notice, identity, hardship, payment difficulty, life support, dispute, ombudsman and concession/credit status before any disconnection order. |
| Third-party payment channels were not reconciled | AGL, Alinta and Origin Centrepay matters | Reconcile Centrepay and other third-party payments against active accounts, closed accounts, credits, refunds and customer notifications. |
| Bill content rules implemented incorrectly | Better Bills review and AER s 37 decision; Victorian best-offer penalties | Treat bill content as regulated output, with automated tests and sample audits for each tariff, plan and customer class. |

## Retail authorisation implications

A new AER-authorised retailer should be able to show:

1. A hardship policy that is implemented in operational systems and not merely approved on paper.
2. Life support workflows covering registration, information packs, distributor notification, planned interruption controls, deregistration and audit.
3. EIC evidence capture for sales, transfers, plan changes, VPP enrolment and controlled-load or data-sharing arrangements.
4. Disconnection gates that block action where hardship, life support, payment plan, complaint, ombudsman or notice conditions are unresolved.
5. Bill rendering and Better Bills tests across all standard and market offers.
6. Ombudsman membership and complaint handling procedures before customer acquisition.
7. Reconciliation for Centrepay, refunds, closed accounts and credits, with escalation for vulnerable customers.
